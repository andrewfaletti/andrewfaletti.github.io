The Thirst Was Never the Problem

Desire, Deception, Divine Provision, and Devotion

In February of 1856, the packet ship John Rutledge struck an iceberg in the North Atlantic and went down. Thirteen people crowded into Lifeboat No. 5, three of them children. They had almost no fresh water. And in every direction, all the way to the horizon, there was nothing but water.

You can probably guess what happened. The thirst became unbearable, and one by one they began to drink the sea. The relief was immediate. It was also a lie. Salt water pulls the water out of your body, so every swallow that promised life actually sped up the dying. Delirium set in. One by one they lost their minds, and one by one they died.

Here is the part I cannot get past. Every one of them was doing the most natural thing in the world. They were thirsty, and there was water everywhere.

That lifeboat is a picture of something James wants us to see. His second chapter of teaching moves through four movements of the heart: desire, deception, divine provision, and devotion. Keep the boat in your mind, because it turns out to describe all four.

Desire: the thirst was never the problem

"Each person is tempted when they are dragged away by their own evil desire and enticed. Then, after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, gives birth to death." James 1:14-15

The phrase "dragged away" is a hunting image. It pictures an animal lured out from the safety of its refuge and into the open, where it can be taken. James is tracing a progression, and notice where it begins. It never starts at sin. It starts at desire aimed at the wrong source.

Desire, then enticement, then sin, then death. Drink by drink. And this is the turn I want you to sit with: the thirst was never the problem. Thirst is good. It is God given and right. The problem was the source. The sea looked like the answer and stood ready with instant relief, and that is exactly what enticement is. Desire aimed at a counterfeit source does not merely fail to satisfy. It accelerates death, and it feels like satisfaction the whole way down.

James says the same thing again a few chapters later:

"What causes fights and quarrels among you? Don't they come from your desires that battle within you? ... You do not have because you do not ask God." James 4:1-3

Read that last line carefully, because it protects us from a mistake. James is not saying desire is evil. He says, "you do not have because you do not ask God." The problem is not desire itself. The problem is detached desire, desire severed from God, drifting toward whatever counterfeit is closest.

Scripture actually speaks about desire in glowing terms. "Hope deferred makes the heart sick, but a desire fulfilled is a tree of life" (Proverbs 13:12). Jesus said, "I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer" (Luke 22:15). Paul wrote, "I have a desire to depart and be with Christ" (Philippians 1:23). It can also go wrong, of course, as when we desire something that belongs to someone else (Romans 7:7). The variable is not the desire. The variable is attachment: is it rooted in God, or cut loose from Him?

I felt this recently in something small. My desire for peace and rest, of all things, started fueling conflict with Ashley. Do we eat dinner at home or go out? Each of us was quietly trying to spend what little margin we had on our own preference. The moment our hearts turned back toward each other, the whole thing reversed. That is the pattern worth learning. Desire, enticed by God's Word instead of the counterfeit, conceives love, and love produces life.

Deception: the mess in the mirror

"Do not merely listen to the word, and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says. Anyone who listens to the word but does not do what it says is like someone who looks at his face in a mirror and, after looking at himself, goes away and immediately forgets what he looks like." James 1:22-24

Here is how desire drifts into deception. We hear God's Word without doing it. We see the mess in the mirror and walk away without responding. James is blunt about it: that is self deception. Hearing without doing, seeing without responding, and then forgetting the whole thing by lunchtime.

But notice what follows the warning. "Whoever looks intently into the perfect law, and continues in it, will be blessed in what they do" (James 1:25). Blessing follows doing. It is the same note the Psalms open with, the person who delights in God's instruction and meditates on it day and night (Psalm 1:1-2).

James later names two kinds of wisdom that grow from these two responses to God's Word (James 3:13-18). Deception produces a disordered life. Wisdom embraced looks like mercy in action.

Selfish ambitionPure and peace loving
Bitter envyConsiderate and sincere
DisorderFull of mercy and good fruit
Every evil practiceSowing peace, reaping righteousness

"Peacemakers who sow in peace reap a harvest of righteousness" (James 3:18). One kind of wisdom fights for itself. The other makes peace and leaves a harvest behind it.

Divine provision: the Father of the heavenly lights

"Every good and perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of the heavenly lights, who does not change like shifting shadows. He chose to give us birth through the word of truth." James 1:17-18

Have you ever been given a genuinely perfect gift? The kind that fit you so exactly it felt like the giver had been paying attention all along? That is the picture James reaches for, and then he stretches it as far as it will go. God gives good gifts. God gives perfect gifts. God gives generously.

Jesus made the same argument. "How much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who ask him" (Matthew 7:11). Luke sharpens it: "How much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him" (Luke 11:13). And unlike every other giver you have ever known, there are no shifting shadows in this One. He does not change, and He does not have moods that determine whether today is a good day to ask.

This reframes the whole lifeboat. Sin is a misdirected search for what only God can provide. The deepest human longing was never really for the created gift in the first place. It was for God Himself. Which means the root deception, underneath desire and beneath every counterfeit, is simply distrusting God's goodness, believing that life can be found somewhere apart from Him.

Devotion: the man who would not drink

"Faith by itself, if it is not accompanied by action, is dead. ... As the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without deeds is dead." James 2:17, 26

When James says faith without works is dead, he is not turning legalistic on us. He is connecting faith to loyalty and devotion. Think of Abraham on the mountain, or Rahab hiding the spies. In both, faith and action worked together, and the faith was made complete by what they did.

So faith is not intellectual assent to a monotheistic worldview. Even the demons believe that much, James says, and they shudder. Real faith is believing that the God of the heavenly lights actually loves you, and has already given you His Son and His Spirit. That kind of faith cannot stay inside your head. It produces mercy, love, and generosity. It transforms us into people who look a little like God. Works are not a performance to earn anything. They are the evidence of where our allegiance already lies. James cares far less about our religious claims than about whether our lives are whole.

Which brings us back to the boat. Nine days in, one man in Lifeboat No. 5 still had not touched the sea. Thomas Nye felt the same killing thirst as everyone else. The same water was right there for him too. And he refused it. Near death but clear minded, he looked up and saw a sail on the horizon. "I felt as though the voyage was done," he later said. "Just before noon I sighted a ship advancing." Of the thirteen, he was the only survivor. (He was not the only person in history to do it. Poon Lim survived 133 days adrift in 1942 by refusing the sea, rationing fresh water, and catching the rain. Same ocean, same thirst, desire tied to the true source, and he lived.)

God is the fresh water

Here is where all four movements land. God is the giver of every good and perfect gift. He is the fresh water. Devotion is refusing the sea even when it is all around you and your body is screaming for it.

Desire connected to the Source produces life. Desire severed from the Source produces death, and it will taste like relief the whole way down.

That is the choice in the boat, and it is the choice in front of us this week. The sea is always right there, always convincing, always immediate. Devotion is the daily refusal to drink it, and the daily turn toward the One who actually satisfies.

If you want to go deeper, I would love for you to read through the book of James yourself over the next two weeks. Take it slowly, a few verses at a time, and watch for these four movements in your own heart.
