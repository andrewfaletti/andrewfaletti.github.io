Reading Through James · July 31, 2026

Wisdom Is Not an Escape Plan

We ask for a way out. God offers something better.

James does not treat trials as an interruption. In chapter one he treats them as the norm. Not a detour, not a sign that something has gone wrong, just the terrain believers walk. And he tells us to consider it joy when we find ourselves in them, because something is being built in us that cannot be built any other way (James 1:2-4).

Then he says something we tend to read past.

"If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given to you." James 1:5

Here is the honest question. What do we think we are asking for?

When I ask God for wisdom in the middle of something hard, most of the time I am asking for a strategy. Give me the good idea. Show me the door. Tell me which way to go so I can get out from under this. Sometimes I am asking for money, or for a material thing, or for the phone call that changes the situation. I am asking God for an exit.

James is not talking about an exit at all.

The deception underneath the desire

Later in chapter one, James traces how sin actually works in us. It starts with desire. Not desire itself as the enemy, but desire that has been distorted and disconnected from God. That desire gets enticed, it conceives, it gives birth to sin, and sin ends in death (James 1:14-15).

The deception buried in that progression is this. We stop believing that the good and perfect things we want only ever come from God. James says every good and perfect gift comes down from above, from the Father of the heavenly lights (James 1:17). From above. Hold onto that phrase, because he comes back to it.

Two kinds of wisdom

By chapter three, James picks the phrase back up. He describes a wisdom made of bitter jealousy and selfish ambition, boasting, lying against the truth. And he says plainly that this one does not come down from above. It is earthly, sensual, demonic (James 3:15). It produces confusion and every evil thing (James 3:16).

That is the atmosphere where distorted desire goes unchecked. Jealousy and selfish ambition are the greenhouse. Evil desire grows there, gets enticed, and ends exactly where James already told us it ends.

Then comes the contrast.

"But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, reasonable, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality and without hypocrisy. And the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace." James 3:17-18

Put the two chapters side by side. Every good and perfect gift comes down from above. The wisdom from above is pure and peaceful and gentle. Ask God for wisdom. Wisdom is the good and perfect gift. It is the best thing He can give you.

Solomon understood this before James wrote a word of it. God handed him a blank check, "Ask what you wish me to give you," and Solomon asked for a discerning heart to lead the people. God commended the request, told him no one before or after would be like him, and then added the riches and honor Solomon had not asked for (1 Kings 3:5-14).

Wisdom herself makes the same claim in Proverbs.

"Take my instruction rather than silver, and knowledge rather than choicest gold. For wisdom is better than jewels, and all that you may desire cannot compare with her." Proverbs 8:10-11

Read that last line with James chapter one in your other hand. All that you may desire cannot compare with her. The very desires that get distorted and go looking elsewhere are outmatched by the gift God is actually offering. And she is not hiding. Wisdom calls out on the heights, at the crossroads, beside the gates at the entrance of the city (Proverbs 8:1-3). We are not straining to hear a whisper. We are asking for the wrong thing while standing in the street where wisdom is shouting.

What wisdom actually produces

Read James 3:17 slowly, because it is not a description of a plan. It is a description of a person.

Pure. It produces a clean heart and clean hands.

Peaceful. It makes you peaceful, and then it makes you a peacemaker. That is the whole point of the next verse. Wisdom produces people who sow peace and make peace (James 3:18).

Gentle. Jesus had the full wisdom of God, and He was gentle and lowly in heart (Matthew 11:29).

Reasonable. Someone you can actually reason with. Someone who stays in the conversation, who keeps themselves in the room, patient and loving, a non-anxious presence even with people who are their enemies. I wrote about that kind of differentiated presence in Knowing Who You Are, if you want the longer version.

Full of mercy. When someone wrongs them, they show mercy. They give grace. They forgive, the way God forgives, because God Himself is full of mercy.

Full of good fruits. What they do bears fruit that lasts (John 15:16).

Without partiality. They are fair. They respect people simply because people are made in the image of God, and they do not treat someone differently based on their merit, their accomplishments, or their net worth.

Without hypocrisy. They are not double minded (James 1:8). They are true to their word in secret and in public. They do not look in the mirror of the word, see a mess, and walk away and do nothing (James 1:23-25). They let it confront them. They are honest about where they are disheveled and where they need work. They deal with the plank in their own eye instead of staying fixated on the speck in everyone else's (Matthew 7:3-5).

That is what wisdom produces.

What we actually need it for

What James is dealing with is that we need wisdom to go through the trials of this life. Wisdom to be in agreement with God and His ways. Discernment to see where we are supposed to hunker down and stay put and find the joy in it, the way Jesus endured the cross for the joy set before Him (Hebrews 12:2). And discernment to see where the issue truly lies, which is usually closer to home than we want it to be. Our own hearts. Our own sinful ways. Our own lusts, our own jealousies, our own covetousness.

Wisdom is not a direction. It is not a set of good ideas. It is not a way into something or a way out of something. It is not an escape plan.

It is heaven's perspective on your life and on the world around you. It is understanding God, who He is and what He is doing in the earth. It is understanding that He is transcendent, above all the rigmarole of our daily lives. That He is the creator of heaven and earth, the Father of the heavenly lights (James 1:17), full of glory, eternal, full of mercy, powerful, loving. That He is not bound by the trappings of this life but sits far above it. And that He is your Father.

Wisdom anchors you to all of that, and it creates a radically differentiated kind of person. Set apart. Holy. Wisdom produces a person set apart for God, not a person with good ideas.

Does God give good ideas? Absolutely. Does He grant success and prosperity in someone's life if He wills it? Absolutely. But that is not the gift James is pointing at.

The gift is a person

The wisdom from above is knit to the Holy Spirit dwelling in us. It is the mind of Christ (1 Corinthians 2:16).

It helps to remember who is writing this letter. James is Jesus' half brother (Matthew 13:55, Galatians 1:19). He grew up in the same house. And when he tells us to ask the Father for wisdom, he is echoing something he heard his brother say. Jesus said that the Father will give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him (Luke 11:13). In Matthew's account of the same teaching, Jesus calls it giving good gifts to those who ask Him (Matthew 7:11). Good gifts. Every good and perfect gift comes down from above.

Those are not two different requests. Ask for wisdom, ask for the Spirit. The Holy Spirit is the wisdom of God, and He is the gift that produces every other good thing in your life.

Which means I have spent a lot of years asking for the wrong thing while using the right word.

Wisdom makes peace.
